Converting Nominal Pipe Size (NPS) and Schedule into OD, wall $t$, ID, and linear weight is daily plant work. Confusing NPS with OD, or STD with Sch 40, drives procurement errors and field fit-up failures.

Look up NPS ½–24 schedules, ID/OD, $W_m$, and MTO totals in the [Pipe Schedule & Dimension](/calculator/pipe-schedule-dimension) calculator.

## Quick Summary (TL;DR)

- **Takeaway:** NPS is a tag, not OD. Use catalog OD and schedule $t$; flow uses $ID = OD - 2t$; pressure design uses $OD$.
- **Governing relation:** Plain-end CS mass $W_m = 0.0246615 \cdot t \cdot (OD - t)$ ($\text{kg/m}$, $\rho = 7{,}850\text{ kg/m}^3$).
- **Code / basis:** ASME B36.10M (CS/alloy) and B36.19M (S-schedules); STD ≠ Sch 40 above NPS 10.

| Item | Field takeaway |
| :--- | :--- |
| **NPS ≤ 12 OD** | OD larger than nominal inch (e.g. NPS 4 = 114.30 mm) |
| **NPS ≥ 14 OD** | OD equals nominal inches (NPS 14 = 355.60 mm) |
| **STD vs Sch 40** | Match through NPS 10; diverge at NPS 12+ |
| **XS vs Sch 80** | Match through NPS 8; diverge above |
| **Worked NPS 8 Sch 80** | OD 219.08 mm · $t$ 12.70 mm · $W_m \approx 64.64\text{ kg/m}$ |
| **Limits** | Catalog nominal wall — apply mill under-tolerance in B31.3 thickness design |

<Callout title="Screening vs. mill certificate" variant="info">
FEK schedule dimensions are **ASME B36 catalog screening**. Confirm heat OD/wall on the mill cert before hydrotest or fit-up. Pressure design must still apply mill under-tolerance (typically $t_m / 0.875$) in the [ASME B31.3 Pipe Thickness](/calculator/pipe-wall-thickness) calculator.
</Callout>

## Pipe schedule dimensions in the field

### ASME B36.10M vs ASME B36.19M
* **ASME B36.10M**: Wrought welded and seamless carbon and alloy steel pipe.
* **ASME B36.19M**: Stainless steel pipe with S-suffix schedules (5S, 10S, 40S, 80S).

| Symbol | Parameter | Units | Description |
| :---: | :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **NPS / DN** | Nominal size | — / $\text{mm}$ | Tag per NPS (in) or ISO 6708 DN (e.g., NPS 4 = DN 100). |
| **OD** | Outside diameter | $\text{mm}$ ($\text{in}$) | Governing OD per B36.10M / B36.19M. |
| **$t$** | Wall thickness | $\text{mm}$ ($\text{in}$) | Nominal catalog wall for the schedule. |
| **ID** | Inside diameter | $\text{mm}$ ($\text{in}$) | Flow bore: $ID = OD - 2t$. |
| **$W_m$** | Plain-end linear weight | $\text{kg/m}$ ($\text{lb/ft}$) | $W_m = 0.0246615 \cdot t \cdot (OD - t)$. |
| **$A_{\text{flow}}$** | Flow area | $\text{mm}^2$ ($\text{in}^2$) | $A_{\text{flow}} = \frac{\pi \cdot ID^2}{4}$. |

## Worked example — NPS 8 Schedule 80 dimensions

### Given Parameters
* **Nominal Size**: NPS 8 (DN 200)
* **Schedule**: Schedule 80 (XS)
* **Material**: ASTM A106 Gr. B ($\rho = 7,850\text{ kg/m}^3$)
* **Fluid**: Water @ 20°C ($\rho_w = 998\text{ kg/m}^3$)

Open the same row in FEK: [Pipe Schedule & Dimension](/calculator/pipe-schedule-dimension) (NPS 8 · Sch 80).

### Step 1 — Table lookup (B36.10M)
$$ OD = 219.08\text{ mm } (8.625\text{ in}) $$
$$ t = 12.70\text{ mm } (0.500\text{ in}) $$

### Step 2 — Inside diameter
$$ ID = 219.08 - 2(12.70) = 193.68\text{ mm } (7.625\text{ in}) $$

### Step 3 — Flow area
$$ A_{\text{flow}} = \frac{\pi \cdot (193.68)^2}{4} = 29,461\text{ mm}^2 = 0.02946\text{ m}^2 $$

### Step 4 — Empty pipe weight
$$ W_{\text{pipe}} = 0.0246615 \times 12.70 \times (219.08 - 12.70) = 64.64\text{ kg/m } (43.43\text{ lb/ft}) $$

### Step 5 — Water-filled weight (rack loading)
* **Water weight**: $W_{\text{water}} = 0.02946 \times 998 = 29.40\text{ kg/m}$
* **Total weight**: $W_{\text{total}} = 64.64 + 29.40 = 94.04\text{ kg/m } (63.19\text{ lb/ft})$

<Callout title="Field callout — filled line weight" variant="tip">
Pipe rack and spring support design must use **filled** line weight (operating fluid or hydrotest water), not empty $W_m$ alone.
</Callout>

## Procurement & engineering traps

### Trap 1 — STD/XS vs Sch 40/80 Divergence
STD and Sch 40 match only for NPS 1/8 through NPS 10. Example NPS 12:

| Designation | Wall Thickness ($t$) |
| :--- | ---: |
| **STD** | $9.53\text{ mm}$ ($0.375\text{ in}$) |
| **Sch 40** | $10.31\text{ mm}$ ($0.406\text{ in}$) |

Ordering NPS 12 STD when the line class requires Sch 40 delivers an under-designed spool.

### Trap 2 — B36.19M Sch 40S vs B36.10M Sch 40
For $\text{NPS} \le 12$, Sch 40 and Sch 40S walls match. At $\text{NPS} \ge 14$, Sch 40S is capped at $9.53\text{ mm}$ while B36.10M Sch 40 continues to grow (NPS 16 Sch 40 = $12.70\text{ mm}$).

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1. Why is NPS 4 OD 114.3 mm, not 101.6 mm?
Historical NPS tags reflect nominal internal capacity, not OD. Only at NPS 14+ does NPS equal OD in inches.

### Q2. Should I use OD or ID for velocity vs pressure calculations?
* **Velocity ($v = Q / A$)**: Inside Diameter ($ID$).
* **ASME B31.3 Pressure Design & Insulation**: Outside Diameter ($OD$).

### Q3. What is the standard mill under-tolerance for pipe?
ASTM A106 / A53 / A312 typically allow $-12.5\%$ on nominal wall. Minimum delivered wall can be $0.875 \times t$ — always account for this in pressure design.

### Q4. Why do some websites list STD = Sch 40 for NPS 12?
That is a common internet shortcut and **wrong for NPS 12+**. FEK and B36.10M keep STD and Sch 40 as separate walls above NPS 10 — verify the schedule tag on the MTO, not a blog table that merges them.
