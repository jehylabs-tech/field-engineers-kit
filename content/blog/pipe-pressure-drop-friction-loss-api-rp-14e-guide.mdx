Friction pressure drop ($\Delta P$) and line velocity govern pump head, control-valve $\Delta P$ budget, and erosion-corrosion risk on process piping. Viscous shear plus wall roughness dissipate mechanical energy along every meter of pipe and every fitting; under-predicting that loss undersizes pumps, while oversizing velocity strips protective scale and shortens carbon-steel life.

| Item | Rule of thumb |
| :--- | :--- |

## 1. Core Engineering Formulas & Parameter Definitions

Hydraulic friction loss for incompressible single-phase flow follows the **Darcy–Weisbach** equation. The Darcy friction factor $f$ is obtained from the **Haaland** explicit approximation to Colebrook–White. Fitting losses enter as **Crane TP-410** equivalent lengths. Erosion screening uses **API RP 14E**. Pipe bore $D$ must be the schedule **inside diameter** (ASME B36.10M / B36.19M).

$$
\Delta P = f \cdot \left( \frac{L_{\text{total}}}{D} \right) \cdot \left( \frac{1}{2} \cdot \rho \cdot v^2 \right)
$$

$$
h_f = \frac{\Delta P}{\rho \cdot g} = f \cdot \left( \frac{L_{\text{total}}}{D} \right) \cdot \left( \frac{v^2}{2g} \right)
$$

$$
\frac{1}{\sqrt{f}} = -1.8 \cdot \log_{10} \left[ \left( \frac{\varepsilon / D}{3.7} \right)^{1.11} + \frac{6.9}{Re} \right]
\quad (Re \ge 2300)
$$

$$
L_{\text{total}} = L + \sum L_{\text{eq}} = L + \sum \left[ \left( \frac{L}{D} \right)_{\text{fitting}} \cdot D \right]
$$

$$
v_c = \frac{C}{\sqrt{\rho}}
\quad (\rho\ \text{in}\ \text{lb/ft}^3,\ v_c\ \text{in}\ \text{ft/s};\ \text{convert to SI as needed})
$$

## 2. Standard Roughness, Crane $L/D$ & Velocity Guidelines

### Absolute Roughness ($\varepsilon$) — Screening Defaults

| Material / Condition | $\varepsilon$ | Notes |
| :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **Stainless / PVC (smooth)** | $0.015\text{ mm}$ ($0.0006\text{ in}$) | Matches calculator “SS / PVC” preset. |
| **New commercial carbon steel** | $0.045\text{ mm}$ ($0.0018\text{ in}$) | ASTM A53 / A106 baseline. |
| **Corroded commercial steel** | $0.15\text{ mm}$ ($0.006\text{ in}$) | Aged plant water / untreated service. |
| **Heavily corroded / scaled** | $0.30\text{ mm}$ ($0.012\text{ in}$) | Conservative long-term friction screen. |

### Crane TP-410 Fitting Equivalent Length Factors ($L/D$)

| Fitting (fully open / installed) | $(L/D)$ | Calculator mapping |
| :--- | ---: | :--- |
| **$90^\circ$ long-radius (LR) elbow** | $30$ | Elbow count × $30$ |
| **$90^\circ$ short-radius (SR) elbow** | $60$ | Not in default UI — apply manually if SR. |
| **$45^\circ$ elbow** | $16$ | Manual add if present. |
| **Full-port gate valve** | $8$ | Gate count × $8$ |
| **Globe valve** | $340$ | Globe count × $340$ |
| **Swing check valve** | $100$ | Manual add if present. |

$$
\sum L_{\text{eq}} = D \cdot \big( 30\,N_{\text{elbow}} + 8\,N_{\text{gate}} + 340\,N_{\text{globe}} + \cdots \big)
$$

### Mill Tolerance Note (ID Sensitivity)

Seamless pipe often carries **$-12.5\%$** mill under-tolerance on wall thickness (e.g. ASTM A106). A thinner wall increases actual ID, which **lowers** $v$ and $\Delta P$ ($\Delta P$ scales roughly as $1/D^5$ in fully rough turbulent flow). Catalog B36 IDs remain the correct **screening** basis unless measured ID is available.

### Recommended Liquid Velocity & Gradient Windows

| Service | Typical velocity | Typical $\Delta P / 100\text{ m}$ | Engineering note |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **Pump suction** | $0.6$–$1.5\text{ m/s}$ ($2$–$5\text{ ft/s}$) | $\le 0.05\text{ bar}/100\text{ m}$ | Preserve NPSHa margin. |
| **Pump discharge / process liquid** | $1.5$–$3.0\text{ m/s}$ ($5$–$10\text{ ft/s}$) | $0.10$–$0.20\text{ bar}/100\text{ m}$ | CAPEX vs pumping OPEX balance. |
| **CS liquid erosion caution** | $\gtrsim 3.5\text{ m/s}$ ($11.5\text{ ft/s}$) | — | Oxide-film stripping risk rises; check API RP 14E $v_c$. |
| **Clean dry gas / HP steam** | $15$–$35\text{ m/s}$ ($50$–$115\text{ ft/s}$) | Case-specific | Compressible methods may be required. |

## 3. Step-by-Step Worked Example

### Field Scenario

Size hydraulics for an **NPS 6 Schedule 40** carbon-steel cooling-water run and confirm API RP 14E continuous-service erosion margin.

| Input | Value |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Fluid** | Water at $20^\circ\text{C}$ ($\rho = 998\text{ kg/m}^3$, $\mu = 1.002\times 10^{-3}\text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s}$) |
| **Pipe** | NPS 6 (DN 150) Sch 40 CS — $\text{ID}\,D = 154.06\text{ mm}$ ($0.15406\text{ m}$) per B36.10M |
| **Flow** | $Q = 120.0\text{ m}^3\text{/h}$ ($0.033333\text{ m}^3\text{/s}$) |
| **Straight length** | $L = 150.0\text{ m}$ |
| **Fittings** | Six $90^\circ$ LR elbows ($L/D = 30$) + two full-port gate valves ($L/D = 8$) |
| **Roughness** | $\varepsilon = 0.045\text{ mm}$ (new commercial steel) |
| **API RP 14E $C$** | $100$ (continuous, solids-free CS) |

### Step 1 — Area and Velocity

$$
A = \frac{\pi}{4} D^2 = \frac{\pi}{4} (0.15406)^2 = 0.018641\text{ m}^2
$$

$$
v = \frac{Q}{A} = \frac{0.033333}{0.018641} = 1.788\text{ m/s}\quad (5.87\text{ ft/s})
$$

### Step 2 — API RP 14E Erosion Limit

Convert density to $\text{lb/ft}^3$: $\rho = 998 \times 0.062428 = 62.30\text{ lb/ft}^3$.

$$
v_c = \frac{100}{\sqrt{62.30}} = 12.67\text{ ft/s} = 3.86\text{ m/s}
$$

Operating $v / v_c = 1.788 / 3.86 \approx 0.46$ — **Safe** under continuous $C = 100$ screening (well below the $0.8\,v_c$ warning band).

### Step 3 — Reynolds Number

$$
Re = \frac{\rho v D}{\mu} = \frac{998 \times 1.788 \times 0.15406}{1.002\times 10^{-3}} = 2.74\times 10^{5}
$$

Fully turbulent ($Re \gg 4000$).

### Step 4 — Haaland Friction Factor

Relative roughness $\varepsilon / D = 0.045 / 154.06 = 2.921\times 10^{-4}$.

$$
\frac{1}{\sqrt{f}} = -1.8\log_{10}\!\left[\left(\frac{2.921\times 10^{-4}}{3.7}\right)^{1.11} + \frac{6.9}{2.74\times 10^{5}}\right] = 7.696
$$

$$
f = (1/7.696)^2 = 0.01689
$$

### Step 5 — Equivalent and Total Length

| Fitting group | Calculation | $L_{\text{eq}}$ |
| :--- | :--- | ---: |
| **6 × LR $90^\circ$ elbows** | $6 \times 30 \times 0.15406$ | $27.73\text{ m}$ |
| **2 × gate valves** | $2 \times 8 \times 0.15406$ | $2.47\text{ m}$ |
| **$\sum L_{\text{eq}}$** | | **$30.20\text{ m}$** |

$$
L_{\text{total}} = 150.0 + 30.20 = 180.20\text{ m}
$$

### Step 6 — Total $\Delta P$ and Head Loss

$$
\frac{1}{2}\rho v^2 = 0.5 \times 998 \times (1.788)^2 = 1{,}595\text{ Pa}
$$

$$
\Delta P = 0.01689 \times \left(\frac{180.20}{0.15406}\right) \times 1{,}595 = 31{,}520\text{ Pa} = 0.315\text{ bar}\quad (4.57\text{ psi})
$$

$$
h_f = \frac{31{,}520}{998 \times 9.81} = 3.22\text{ m of fluid}
$$

**Conclusion:** NPS 6 Sch 40 at $120\text{ m}^3\text{/h}$ yields **$v = 1.79\text{ m/s}$**, **$f = 0.0169$**, and **$\Delta P = 0.315\text{ bar}$** including fittings — comfortably inside API RP 14E continuous limits. Straight-only $\Delta P / 100\text{ m}$ would be lower than the total $\Delta P$ scaled to $100\text{ m}$; always keep that distinction clear when reading calculator outputs.

## 5. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1. Why use Haaland instead of Colebrook–White?

Colebrook–White is implicit and needs iteration. Haaland is explicit and typically stays within about **$1$–$1.5\%$** of Colebrook $f$ — usually smaller than the uncertainty in field roughness ($\varepsilon$). The FieldEngineersKit pressure-drop engine uses Haaland for turbulent flow and $f = 64/Re$ for laminar.

### Q2. Darcy vs Fanning friction factor — which does this article use?

**Darcy** ($f$). Chemical-engineering texts often quote Fanning $f_F$ where $f = 4\,f_F$. Using Fanning numbers in a Darcy equation under-predicts $\Delta P$ by a factor of four.

### Q3. Does $\Delta P / 100\text{ m}$ include elbows and valves?

**No.** In the calculator, $\Delta P / 100\text{ m}$ (or $/100\text{ ft}$) is the **straight-pipe** unit gradient. Total (hero) $\Delta P$ adds Crane equivalent lengths. Comparing two NPS options on $\Delta P/100$ is fine; pump head must use **total** $\Delta P$ including fittings, elevation, and control-valve allowance.

### Q4. How should API RP 14E $C$ be selected?

For **continuous** solids-free liquid service on carbon steel, $C = 100$ is the usual screen; intermittent or stainless systems often use $C = 150$–$200$. The formula is empirical — sand, two-phase flow, or corrosive chemistry can require lower allowable velocities than $v_c$. Always confirm against the current API RP 14E text and owner standards.
