## Core formulas & parameter definitions

### Liquid US $C_v$ (Valve Cv path)

FEK converts metric liquid inputs to US customary units, then applies:

$$
C_v = Q_{\mathrm{gpm}} \sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{SG}}{\Delta P_{\mathrm{psi}}}}
$$

with $Q_{\mathrm{gpm}} = Q_{\mathrm{m}^3/\mathrm{h}} \times 4.402867655$ and $\Delta P_{\mathrm{psi}} = \Delta P_{\mathrm{bar}} \times 14.5037738$. For incompressible liquid, **gauge $\Delta P$ equals absolute $\Delta P$**.

Approximate Kv (not shown in the Cv app):

$$
K_v \approx \frac{C_v}{1.156}
$$

### Liquid cavitation limit (choked-screening path)

Pressures here are **absolute**:

$$
r_c = 0.96 - 0.28\sqrt{\frac{P_v}{P_c}}
$$

$$
\Delta P_{\mathrm{cav}} = F_L^{2}\,(P_1 - r_c\,P_v)
$$

| Condition | FEK state |
| :--- | :--- |
| $P_2 \le P_v$ | **Flashing** |
| $\Delta P \ge \Delta P_{\mathrm{cav}}$ | **Cavitating / choked liquid** |
| otherwise | **Normal liquid** |

### Gas note ($x_T$)

Gas / vapor choking uses $x = \Delta P / P_1$ against $F_k\cdot x_T$. That is a separate FEK path — do not apply $x_T$ to water or $F_L$ to compressible gas.

### Parameter definitions

| Symbol | Name | Unit | Description |
| :---: | :--- | :---: | :--- |
| $C_v$ | Required US flow coefficient | — | FEK hero (liquid/gas screening) |
| $C_{v,\mathrm{sel}}$ | Catalog full-open $C_v$ | — | Adequate when $C_v \le C_{v,\mathrm{sel}}$ |
| $Q$ | Volumetric flow | $\mathrm{m}^3/\mathrm{h}$ or GPM | Liquid capacity basis |
| $P_1$, $P_2$ | Upstream / downstream pressure | bar g or bar abs | **Gauge** in Cv tool · **absolute** in cavitation tool |
| $\Delta P$ | Valve differential | bar / psi | $P_1 - P_2$ |
| $F_L$ | Liquid pressure recovery factor | — | Trim-dependent (globe single ≈ 0.90) |
| $x_T$ | Gas differential pressure ratio factor | — | Gas choke limit (sister tool) |
| $P_v$, $P_c$ | Vapor / critical pressure | bar abs | Water @ 25 °C: $0.0317$ / $220.64$ |
| $r_c$ | Critical pressure ratio factor | — | From $P_v/P_c$ |
| $F_P$ | Piping geometry factor | — | FEK assumes $F_P = 1$ |

## How to calculate control valve Cv (liquid service worked example)

### Cooling-water loop — worked example

Cooling-water flow control. Water at $25^\circ\mathrm{C}$, $Q = 120\text{ m}^3/\mathrm{h}$, absolute line pressures $P_1 = 6.0\text{ bar abs}$, $P_2 = 3.5\text{ bar abs}$ ($\Delta P = 2.5\text{ bar}$), globe single-seat trim $F_L = 0.90$.

| Input | Value |
| :--- | :--- |
| Fluid | Water, $\mathrm{SG} = 1.00$ |
| $Q$ | $120\text{ m}^3/\mathrm{h}$ |
| Absolute $P_1$ / $P_2$ | $6.0$ / $3.5\text{ bar abs}$ (cavitation tool) |
| Cv-tool gauge example | $P_1 = 6.0\text{ bar g}$, $P_2 = 3.5\text{ bar g}$ → same $\Delta P = 2.5\text{ bar}$ |
| $\Delta P$ | $2.5\text{ bar}$ ($36.26\text{ psi}$) |
| $F_L$ | $0.90$ (globe · single seated) |
| $P_v$ / $P_c$ | $0.0317$ / $220.64\text{ bar abs}$ (FEK @ 25 °C) |

### Step 1 — Required US $C_v$ (capacity)

$$
Q_{\mathrm{gpm}} = 120 \times 4.402867655 = 528.34\text{ GPM}
$$

$$
\Delta P_{\mathrm{psi}} = 2.5 \times 14.5037738 = 36.26\text{ psi}
$$

$$
C_v = 528.34\sqrt{\frac{1.0}{36.26}} = \mathbf{87.74}
$$

Reference $\mathrm{Kv} \approx 87.74 / 1.156 \approx 75.9$. A catalog $C_{v,\mathrm{sel}}$ near **100–120** is a common headroom band (enter the actual OEM $C_v$ in the app).

### Step 2 — Critical pressure ratio $r_c$

$$
r_c = 0.96 - 0.28\sqrt{\frac{0.0317}{220.64}} = 0.9566
$$

### Step 3 — Cavitation limit $\Delta P_{\mathrm{cav}}$

$$
\Delta P_{\mathrm{cav}} = 0.90^{2}\,(6.0 - 0.9566\cdot 0.0317) = 4.835\text{ bar}
$$

### Step 4 — Compare operating $\Delta P$

$$
\Delta P = 2.5\text{ bar} < 4.835\text{ bar} \implies \textbf{normal liquid (no cavitation)}
$$

Margin $\Delta P / \Delta P_{\mathrm{cav}} \approx 0.52$.

| Check | Engine result |
| :--- | :--- |
| Required $C_v$ | **87.74** |
| $\Delta P_{\mathrm{cav}}$ | **4.835 bar** |
| Flow state | **Normal liquid** |
| Suggested $C_{v,\mathrm{sel}}$ band | **100–120** (headroom guidance) |

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1. How do I size liquid Cv and then check cavitation?

First compute required **US $C_v$** from $Q$ and $\Delta P$ in [Valve Cv Sizing](/calculator/valve-cv-sizing). Then, with **absolute** $P_1$, $P_v$, $P_c$, and trim $F_L$, verify $\Delta P < F_L^2(P_1 - r_c P_v)$ in [Control Valve Choked Flow](/calculator/control-valve-choked-screening). Capacity and cavitation are separate screens — FEK does not fold $F_L$ into the Cv hero.

### Q2. What is the difference between $F_L$ pressure recovery factor and $x_T$ choked flow limit?

**$F_L$** is the **liquid** pressure-recovery factor used in $\Delta P_{\mathrm{cav}}$. **$x_T$** is the **gas/vapor** differential-pressure ratio factor used with $x = \Delta P/P_1$ and $F_k$. Do not apply $x_T$ to water service or $F_L$ to compressible gas choking.

### Q3. How does valve sizing affect downstream pipeline pressure drop and noise?

A larger valve $\Delta P$ lowers required $C_v$ but raises jet velocity, cavitation risk, and noise. After capacity and $F_L$ checks, screen $L_{p,1\mathrm{m}}$ with [Control Valve Noise](/calculator/control-valve-noise) and close the line hydraulic balance separately (pipe $\Delta P$ is not the valve $\Delta P$).

### Q4. Why does FieldEngineersKit report a different Cv than $Q/1.17\sqrt{\mathrm{SG}/\Delta P}$?

Because FEK implements the **US $C_v$** definition after converting to **GPM** and **psi**. The $/1.17$ shortcut understates $C_v$ for this water duty (**87.7 vs ≈ 64.9**). Prefer the ISA/IEC unit-consistent path — or manufacturer software for purchase.
