## Why pipe insulation thickness and surface temperature matter

Personnel burns, energy waste, and condensate formation are three different insulation jobs. This article focuses on **hot-service heat-loss and personnel-protection screening**: steady radial conduction through the insulation annulus plus outer convection/radiation, iterated until jacket $T_s$ and $Q$ converge.

Field failure modes look like: bare or thin jackets above **60 °C** on walkways and valve clusters; “catalog thickness” copied from another duty without wind or emissivity; and spreadsheet $k$ frozen at room temperature while $T_m$ is 150 °C. Use [Piping Insulation Thickness & Heat Loss](/calculator/insulation-heat-loss/4inch-mineral-wool-50mm) for the NPS 4 mineral-wool starting point, [ASME B31.3 Pipe Thickness](/calculator/pipe-wall-thickness) when schedule (and OD/wall mass context) changes, and [Steam Turbine Power & SSC](/calculator/steam-turbine-power-ssc) when steam-line heat loss feeds plant heat-rate screening.

<Callout title="Screening vs code / OEM / CUI design" variant="info">
This guide and the FEK calculator implement an **ASTM C680 / ISO 12241–aligned** steady-state radial heat-loss and surface-temperature **screen**, with personnel Pass/Fail at **$T_s \le 60\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$** (ASTM **C1055** ~5 s contact guidance). They are **not** a substitute for a certified ASTM C680 report, manufacturer $k(T)$ curves, project jacket emissivity, or site wind turbulence. **Anti-sweat (dew-point / condensation)** and **CUI (corrosion under insulation)** duties need separate moisture, cladding, and often cellular-glass / aerogel material design — do not treat a personnel Pass as an anti-sweat or CUI Pass. ## Core formulas & parameter definitions

### Linear heat loss (steady radial)

$$
Q = \frac{T_h - T_a}{R_{\mathrm{tot}}}
$$

$$
R_{\mathrm{tot}} = R_{\mathrm{ins}} + R_{\mathrm{conv}}
= \frac{\ln(r_2/r_1)}{2\pi k} + \frac{1}{2\pi r_2 h_o}
$$

with outer film $h_o = h_c + h_r$.

### Outer film coefficients (FEK)

Natural convection (horizontal cylinder, SI screening form used in FEK):

$$
h_{c,\mathrm{nat}} = 1.32\left(\frac{T_s - T_a}{d_2}\right)^{0.25}
$$

Mild outdoor forced term (intentionally soft so light wind does not erase the C680 natural formula):

$$
h_{c,\mathrm{forced}} \approx \frac{2.2\,v^{0.6}}{d_2^{0.4}}\quad (v>0.05\text{ m/s})
$$

Churchill cube-root blend:

$$
h_c = \left(h_{c,\mathrm{nat}}^3 + h_{c,\mathrm{forced}}^3\right)^{1/3}
$$

Radiation linearized about $T_s$:

$$
h_r = \varepsilon\,\sigma\,\frac{T_s^4 - T_a^4}{T_s - T_a}
$$

($\sigma = 5.670374419\times 10^{-8}\text{ W/m}^2\cdot\text{K}^4$; temperatures absolute in the $T^4$ terms.)

FEK iterates $T_s$ until the heat leaving through $R_{\mathrm{tot}}$ matches the outer film balance $Q = 2\pi r_2 h_o (T_s - T_a)$.

### Mineral-wool conductivity $k(T_m)$ (ASTM C547–style screen)

Mean insulation temperature $T_m = (T_h + T_s)/2$. FEK mineral wool:

$$
k(T_m) = 0.032 + 1.05\times 10^{-4}\,T_m + 2.2\times 10^{-7}\,T_m^2
\quad[\text{W/m·K},\ T_m\text{ in }^\circ\text{C}]
$$

Other FEK materials use parallel ASTM C533 / C552 / C591–style screening polynomials in the same calculator engine.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### What personnel protection surface temperature limit does FEK use?

ASTM **C1055** screening for brief (~5 s) contact is implemented as **$T_s \le 60\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$ (140 °F)**. Higher jacket temperature is flagged **Fail** — increase thickness, add personnel guards, or reroute the walkway. Do **not** invent a lower $\varepsilon$ to force a Pass: bright aluminum ($\varepsilon\approx 0.2$) runs **hotter** at the jacket than a painted finish ($\varepsilon\approx 0.9$) under the same FEK film model. Longer contact, moisture, or metal tools against the jacket can still injure below that screen; treat 60 °C as a project Pass gate, not a guarantee of “safe to lean on.”

### How does a pipe heat loss calculation ASTM C680 screen work in FEK?

FEK builds $R_{\mathrm{ins}}=\ln(r_2/r_1)/(2\pi k)$ with $k(T_m)$ from the material polynomial, adds $R_{\mathrm{conv}}=1/(2\pi r_2 h_o)$ with natural/forced convection blend plus radiation, then iterates $T_s$ until $Q=(T_h-T_a)/R_{\mathrm{tot}}$ matches the outer film. That is the same structure as ASTM **C680** / ISO **12241** steady radial practice — still a **screen**, not a certified lab report.

### Why does mineral wool insulation thermal conductivity in FEK differ from $k\approx 0.045\text{ W/m·K}$?

Because conductivity rises with mean temperature. FEK mineral wool is $k=0.032+1.05\times 10^{-4}T_m+2.2\times 10^{-7}T_m^2$. At the worked $T_m\approx 148\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$, $k\approx\mathbf{0.0524}\text{ W/m·K}$. Spreadsheets that freeze $0.045$ under-predict heat loss and over-predict how cool the jacket will be.

### Why don’t FEK $T_s$ / $Q$ match some internet thickness charts?

Charts differ on $\varepsilon$ (painted **0.9** vs aluminum **~0.2**), wind model aggressiveness, whether $k$ is evaluated at $T_m$, and pipe OD basis. FEK asserts the engine rows in this article (e.g. NPS 4 · MW · 50 mm · 250 °C · 1.5 m/s · $\varepsilon=0.2$ → **$T_s\approx 46.6\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$**, **$Q\approx 106.6\text{ W/m}$**). Confirm manufacturer $k(T)$ and jacket finish before locking MTO thickness.
