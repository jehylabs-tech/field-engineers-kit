Field crews often search for a **bolt tension calculation** when they really need two linked numbers: **target preload (tension)** in each stud and the **wrench torque** that produces it under a stated nut factor $K$. **ASME B16.5** fixes stud count and size; **ASME PCC-1** governs assembly practice (lubricant, multi-pass sequence, and friction-aware torque).

## Quick Summary (TL;DR)

- **Takeaway:** Tension $F$ is the design intent; torque $T$ is the field control — linked by $T = K \cdot D \cdot F$ when friction is known.
- **Governing relation:** $F = \sigma_b \cdot A_s$; FEK screening uses moly table $T = T_{\mathrm{table}} \cdot (K/0.13) \cdot f_{\mathrm{grade}}$.
- **Code / basis:** ASME PCC-1 assembly · B16.5 stud geometry · FEK moly $K_{\mathrm{ref}} = 0.13$ (A193 B7 baseline).

| Item | Field takeaway |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Bolt tension** | Axial preload $F$ in the stud (lbf or kN) |
| **Bolt torque** | Applied nut wrench reading $T$ (ft·lbf or N·m) |
| **Nut factor $K$** | Moly ≈ 0.13 · PTFE ≈ 0.11 · dry ≈ 0.20 |
| **FEK table path** | NPS × class → $T_{\mathrm{table}}$ (moly B7) → rescale by $K$ and grade |
| **FEK rounds** | 30% → 60% → 100% star → 100% circular |
| **Worked FEK row** | NPS 6 Class 300 · 12 × ¾″ · moly B7 → **$T = 366\ \mathrm{N·m}$** |
| **Dry rescale** | $366 \times (0.20/0.13) \approx 563\ \mathrm{N·m}$ — do not use moly $T$ on dry threads |

## Bolt tension vs bolt torque

**Tension (preload)** seats the gasket and resists hydrostatic end force. **Torque** is what calibrated wrenches read. They are not interchangeable labels:

$$
T = K \cdot D \cdot F
$$

$$
F = \sigma_b \cdot A_s
$$

Raising $K$ at fixed $T$ **cuts** preload roughly as $1/K$. That is why a moly table value applied to dry threads under-loads the joint.

The [Bolt Torque calculator](/calculator/bolt-torque-tensioning) is a **B16.5 + PCC-1-style table lookup** with $K$ / grade rescale. It does **not** solve live $F = \sigma_b \cdot A_s$ inside the hero. Hand calc $T = K \cdot D \cdot F$ and table $T$ can differ — the **owner PCC-1 appendix** governs critical joints.
</Callout>

## 1. Core formulas & nut factor

### 1.1 Tensile stress area (Unified inch)

$$
A_s = \frac{\pi}{4}\left(D - \frac{0.9743}{n}\right)^2
$$

### 1.2 FEK screening torque (moly table)

Stored $T_{\mathrm{table}}$ assumes moly anti-seize at $K_{\mathrm{ref}} = 0.13$ and A193 B7:

$$
T = T_{\mathrm{table}} \cdot \frac{K}{0.13} \cdot f_{\mathrm{grade}}
$$

| Lubricant / grade | FEK preset |
| :--- | :--- |
| Moly anti-seize | $K = 0.13$ |
| PTFE / coated | $K = 0.11$ |
| Dry / lightly oiled | $K = 0.20$ |
| A193 B7 | $f_{\mathrm{grade}} = 1.00$ |
| A193 B8 / B8M Class 2 | $f_{\mathrm{grade}} = 0.85$ |

Annealed **B8 Class 1** is **not** offered — never apply B7 table torque to Class 1 studs.

### 1.3 Pass protocol (same as Sequence tool)

| Round | Fraction of $T$ | Pattern |
| :---: | :---: | :--- |
| 1 | 30% | Star / cross |
| 2 | 60% | Star / cross |
| 3 | 100% | Star / cross |
| 4 | 100% | Circular check |

## 2. Worked example — NPS 6 Class 300 (match the calculator)

| Input | Value |
| :--- | :--- |
| Flange | NPS 6 Class 300 RF |
| Studs | 12 × ¾″ (M20) A193 B7 |
| Lubricant | Moly ($K = 0.13$) |

### Step 1 — Read $T_{\mathrm{table}}$

From the FEK B16.5 / PCC-1 screening table (calculator hero):

$$
T_{\mathrm{table}} = 366\ \mathrm{N·m}\ (270\ \mathrm{ft·lbf})
$$

Open the live row: [NPS 6 Class 300 Bolt Torque](/calculator/bolt-torque-tensioning/6-inch-class-300).

### Step 2 — Apply $K$ and grade

$$
T = 366 \cdot \frac{0.13}{0.13} \cdot 1.00 = 366\ \mathrm{N·m}
$$

B8 Class 2: $366 \times 0.85 = 311\ \mathrm{N·m}$.

### Step 3 — Round 1–4 wrench targets

| Round | Target |
| :---: | :--- |
| 1 | $0.30 \times 366 = 110\ \mathrm{N·m}$ |
| 2 | $0.60 \times 366 = 220\ \mathrm{N·m}$ |
| 3 / 4 | $366\ \mathrm{N·m}$ |

Star order for this joint (12-bolt): use the [12-bolt star Sequence Generator](/calculator/flange-bolt-tightening-sequence/12-bolt-star).

### Step 4 — Dry-thread rescale (same preload intent)

$$
T_{\mathrm{dry}} = 366 \cdot \frac{0.20}{0.13} \approx 563\ \mathrm{N·m}\ (415\ \mathrm{ft·lbf})
$$

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1. How do I do a bolt tension calculation for a flange?

1. Get stud size and count from **B16.5** (or the FEK Bolt Torque joint row).  
2. Choose target bolt stress $\sigma_b$ per the owner appendix.  
3. Compute $A_s$, then $F = \sigma_b \cdot A_s$.  
4. Convert to wrench torque with $T = K \cdot D \cdot F$ using the **actual** lubricant $K$.  
5. Or, for FEK screening, read $T_{\mathrm{table}}$ and rescale — [Bolt Torque & Tensioning](/calculator/bolt-torque-tensioning).

### Q2. Is bolt torque the same as bolt tension?

**No.** Tension is axial preload $F$; torque is the wrench reading $T$. They are linked only when $K$ (and geometry) are known: $T = K \cdot D \cdot F$.

### Q3. Why does dry makeup need a higher torque?

Friction rises ($K \approx 0.20$ vs moly $0.13$). For the same intended preload, wrench torque must rise by about $0.20/0.13 \approx 1.54\times$. Applying the moly number on dry threads **under-tensions** the gasket.

### Q4. When should I use hydraulic tensioning instead of torque?

Prefer tensioning for **large studs (about $D \ge 1\text{-}1/2$″)**, **Class 900+**, or **critical services** — axial load is applied without relying on nut factor $K$.

### Q5. Where is the star / torque pattern?

For this worked joint use the [12-bolt star Sequence Generator](/calculator/flange-bolt-tightening-sequence/12-bolt-star). For a 20-stud circle open [20-bolt star](/calculator/flange-bolt-tightening-sequence/20-bolt-star). Rounds 1–3 use star/cross; Round 4 is circular at 100% $T$.
