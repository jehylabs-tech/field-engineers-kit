An **NC calculator** rates room noise against **Noise Criterion (NC)** curves: enter octave-band sound pressure levels $L_p(f_i)$, find the lowest NC curve that is nowhere exceeded (ANSI/ASA S12.2 **tangent** method), then compare to an ASHRAE space maximum for Pass/Fail.

## Quick Summary (TL;DR)

- **Takeaway:** NC = lowest clear NC-15…65 curve under the measured octave-band spectrum; Pass when NC ≤ ASHRAE space max.
- **Governing relation:** $L_p(f_i) \le L_{\mathrm{NC}}(f_i)$ for all bands $f_i \in \{63,\ldots,8000\}\text{ Hz}$.
- **Code / basis:** ANSI/ASA S12.2 · ASHRAE HVAC Applications space maxima.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1. What is an NC calculator?

A tool that computes a **Noise Criterion (NC)** rating from octave-band SPL using the **ANSI/ASA S12.2** tangent method, then checks an **ASHRAE** space limit. FEK: [Noise Criterion Rating Calculator](/calculator/noise-criterion/control-room-nc35).

### Q2. Is estimated dBA exact?

**No.** Screening uses **dBA ≈ NC + 5…8 dB**. Use a calibrated A-weighted measurement for compliance records.

### Q3. When should I use RC or NCB instead?

For **low-frequency rumble** or **tonal** complaints, use **RC** or **NCB** — NC alone can miss those modes.
